# Complementary work

The archive itself is free and stays free: published tables, scripts, certificates and
the protocol. This page lists work that can be commissioned alongside it, so that the
line between published evidence and paid assistance is visible.

## 1. Data quality assistance

A benchmark or vendor supplies its evaluation records; the same export and verify
pipeline is run against them before publication, and the report states cell counts,
thin-cell share, correlation, and the zero-failure bound the data can support. The
benchmark decides what to publish. The archive does not edit the numbers and does not
withhold a result.

## 2. Claim verification assistance

A vendor statement — "first", "89% success", "zero teleoperation" — is checked against
the record the vendor is willing to show: which benchmark, which cells, how many
attempts, whether repeats were counted. Output is a certificate at the level the
evidence supports (L1, L2 or L3, see `CERTIFICATE_LEVELS.md`), including the case where
the claim cannot be supported at any level.

## 3. Making a new capability comparable

A lab ships a capability that has no place on any existing leaderboard: a new contact
behaviour, a recovery routine, a skill that only shows up over hours. The problem is not
that the result is weak; it is that nobody can tell whether it is a result.

What this produces: a task definition with a denominator, an attempt count, the judging
rule, the baseline it is measured against, and intervals at the sample size actually
available. The output is a record another lab can compare against, not a price and not a
ranking. Whether a market prices the capability is a separate question this archive has no
standing to answer; whether the claim is comparable at all, it can answer.

## 4. Statistics for people who do not do statistics

Many of the people who most need a defensible number are not statisticians: graduate
students with a few dozen trials per arm, teachers with class-level data, analysts handed a
spreadsheet they did not build. The failure is rarely malice; it is a denominator that was
never defined, a denominator that moved, or a difference smaller than the sample can
resolve.

What is offered: a template and a self-check page that say what has to be fixed before
numbers mean anything, plus a worked example that runs in a browser. Free, no account.

What is refused: writing anyone's results, choosing an analysis after seeing the data,
trimming a sample to reach a threshold, or putting this archive's name on a manuscript as a
co-author. The line is that this archive can tell a party whether its claim is checkable;
it does not manufacture the claim.

## 5. Deployment evidence review

For a deployed fleet, the 17-field deployment template is filled from operational
records and audited: Wilson interval on the success rate, intervention rate, and an
explicit check that the denominator is stated and interventions count as failures.
Output describes the deployment record, not the robot.

## Boundaries

- No paid ranking, no sponsored tier position, no delaying a release for a client.
- No ghost analysis: a commissioned report checks a party's own record, and the party keeps
  authorship of its claims.
- The archive audits records, not people. A party that brings its logs is treated as
  someone making a claim checkable, not as a defendant.
- Commissioned work is disclosed in the release it touches.
- A commissioned certificate uses the same layers and the same wording as a
  self-initiated one; being paid changes who asked, not what is verified.
- No safety certification, no regulatory opinion, no warranty.
