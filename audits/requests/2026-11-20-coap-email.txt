Dear authors,

We maintain the EEO Archive, a public archive that republishes per-attempt records of robot
evaluations and checks the numbers published on top of them for reproducibility and
comparability. Your paper reports results on RoboDojo, which is also one of the boards the
archive holds; we cover six of its 42 tasks.

We recomputed the Overall column of your RoboDojo results table (Table 2 in the HTML version) from the five domain columns. In all 24 rows the
reported Overall equals the unweighted mean of the five domain success rates, reproducing
within 0.03 percentage points, so the table is arithmetically consistent. Two observations
came out of the recomputation.

First, the domains hold unequal task counts (Memory 6, Precision 8, Open 8, Generalization 12,
Long-Horizon 8). A task-weighted mean differs from the reported one: COAP 68.87 against
70.24, PhysicalRSI 29.15 against 31.38, DM0.5 17.20 against 19.34. Whether the macro mean or
the weighted mean is the intended headline is a choice, but the choice moves the number by
one to two points and is not stated in the text.

Second, from the five aggregates alone the 95% interval around 70.24 is about ±11 pp. Applying
the archive's RoboDojo intra-cluster correlation (0.5013) to the design described in your
Section 4 — 42 tasks, three seeds, 50 layouts each, 6,300 episodes — gives about ±10 pp. The
two routes agree, which suggests the aggregate route does not overstate precision, but both
remain approximations.

Neither observation is a criticism of the result. Both stop at the same place: the paper
reports aggregates, and aggregates cannot be checked against the episodes they summarise.

We would therefore ask for four things, in descending order of how much they would settle:

1. A per-task table of 42 rows: task name, domain, number of attempts, number of successes,
   and the success rule applied. This alone lets any reader reproduce the five domain values
   and the Overall column, and settles the weighting question.

2. A run manifest, one row per evaluated run: task id, seed, layout id, library or code
   version used, date, and outcome, with the failure stage or reason where the run failed.
   Videos and trajectories are not needed; identifiers and outcomes are enough to check
   aggregation and to measure between-task variance.

3. The success rule for partial completions: whether a task counts as failed unless it
   completes, or whether the benchmark's progress score is used; and how the reattempt
   policy described in your breakdown of failed validation episodes interacts with that rule.

4. The library version that produced the reported table, so the numbers attach to a commit
   rather than to a development process.

How we would use the material: we would recompute the headline from your record, report
whether it matches, and cite the paper together with the release. We would not redistribute
your logs; the archive publishes derived tables and hashes, not source records, and the same
policy would apply here. If a discrepancy appeared, we would send it to you before publishing
anything, and we would publish your reply alongside it if you wished.

If releasing logs is not possible, the per-task table alone would raise this check from the
aggregate level to the table level, and we would record that level with the constraint stated
rather than treat the paper as unverifiable.

The note we wrote from the published table is at
https://github.com/Embodied-Evaluation-Observatory/EEO-Archive/blob/main/audits/2026-11-20-coap-2610.12369.md
and we are glad to correct it if we have misread anything.

Thank you for considering this.
